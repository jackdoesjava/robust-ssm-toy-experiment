Notes
- RMSE and CRPS: means over 200 test sequences of 1000 steps, ± the half-width of a 95% interval for the mean. Coverage and width: means. All over steps 1-500 (before the jump).
- Delay: steps after the jump until the error averaged over sequences is below 1 (80% of the jump recovered); 0 means already at the first step after it. Its interval resamples sequences. RMSE after the jump: steps 501-550.
- The ± in a row is for that row alone. Every row is scored on the same test sequences, and each paired line gives a difference its own interval. Between filters that behave alike, such as the robust filter and input-dependent noise from y_t, that interval is often much narrower than either row's (about ±0.001 in RMSE before the jump; wider with bursts or a fast state). Between filters that behave differently, such as the robust filter and one R, it can be as wide as the narrower row's ±, or wider; so can the past-only pair, which is the robust filter against one R wherever tuning switched its noise off. Judge differences by the paired lines, not by the printed digits.
- Parameters were fitted on 100 training and 100 validation sequences without a jump, using observations only (see the docstring): the one-R maximum-likelihood filter by the likelihood of the training sequences, every other tuned filter by the CRPS of the one-step forecast N(m_{t|t-1}, P_{t|t-1} + R), with the base R, on the validation sequences. Every filter knows Q and the prior on x_0. Kalman, R = 1 is given the true clean noise level.
- The Bayes filter knows the true outlier model, including the test-time outlier rate when it differs from the training data, but not the jump, so it has the lowest expected error before the jump only. Where its model has outliers, it first takes the jump for outliers, like the robust and input-dependent filters; with bursts, which it expects to come in runs, it does so for longer.
- The input-dependent noise R_t = R + d_t^2 / c^2 equals R / w_t^2 for an IMQ weight on d_t, the distance from y_t to the median of the previous k readings; the robust weight uses e_t, the distance from the filter's prediction.
- (past only): R_t = R + d_{t-1}^2 / c^2, which uses y_1, ..., y_{t-1} only. It cannot tell whether y_t itself is an outlier, only expect one after an outlier, which can help when outliers come in bursts. Its forecast N(m_{t|t-1}, P_{t|t-1} + R_t) is a proper density for y_t, which it would not be with d_t in place of d_{t-1}; but like every filter it was tuned on the forecast with the base R.
- With c = 2 fixed the two weights are not equally strong: d_t is measured in units of sqrt(R) and e_t in units of sqrt(P_{t|t-1} + R), and in those units d_t is the larger, so the input-dependent weight down-weights more. Their paired line mixes the mechanism with the strength.
- Robust + input-dependent R_t, both versions: R, the robust c and the noise's c and k were tuned together, over a grid that includes both filters alone. The weight is measured against P_{t|t-1} + R_t. Noise from d_t already grows with the reading, so where e_t is close to d_t the weight stays above about (1 + noise c^2 / robust c^2)^(-1/2); noise from past readings leaves the weight free.
